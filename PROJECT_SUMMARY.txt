───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  CENTRAL QUESTION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  How can operational evidence be produced, kept intact, and used to
  reconstruct what actually happened and attribute responsibility — in
  decentralized autonomous agent systems — independently of runtime
  monitoring and policy enforcement?

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  FIVE FUNCTIONS (INITIAL SCOPE)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Fn1 — Evidence Capture       How is evidence about an action, in its
                               context, captured so that it can be
                               examined later?
  Fn2 — Evidence Integrity     How is evidence integrity ensured, and how
                               is tampering or deletion detected?
  Fn3 — Execution              How is the execution trace — actions,
       Reconstruction          transformations, and delegations — rebuilt,
                               including when records are incomplete?
  Fn4 — Responsibility         How is responsibility attributed to an
       Attribution             agent, principal, or delegation chain in
                               a defensible way?
  Fn5 — Audit Verification     How can an independent party verify
                               evidence and issue an audit verdict without
                               assuming trust in the auditor?

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ARCHITECTURAL HYPOTHESIS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A potentially independent architectural gap may exist between runtime
  monitoring, policy enforcement, and identity: the operational management
  of audit evidence — its capture, integrity, reconstruction, attribution,
  and independent verification — across decentralized autonomous agents.

  This hypothesis is falsifiable.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  FALSIFICATION CONDITIONS (A-F1 TO A-F4)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A-F1  Non-redundancy            Not falsified (with reservation)
  A-F2  Distinction from          Not falsified (with reservation) -- MOST FRAGILE
        Runtime + Git
  A-F3  Decentralizability        Not falsified (with reservation)
  A-F4  Observable benefit        Untested

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  INDEPENDENCE DECISION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Classification : (E) undetermined

  The argument is insufficient to accept AetherAudit as an independent
  architectural dimension, and insufficient to reject it. A test is needed.

  The project is paused at Phase 4. Phase 5 (Specification) is not started.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  WHAT THIS PROJECT DOES NOT CLAIM
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  - That AetherAudit is an independent architectural dimension.
  - That the five functions are complete.
  - That the boundaries with AetherRuntime, AetherPolicy, AetherIdentity,
    and the other dimensions are sufficient.
  - That the gap is genuine.
  - That implementation should begin.
